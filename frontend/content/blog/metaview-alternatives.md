Metaview records interviews and generates notes. For teams whose bottleneck is documentation, it solves a real problem. But for most small hiring teams, the bottleneck is earlier: too many applicants, too little time to read them, and no clear way to move the best ones forward.

If you are evaluating Metaview and wondering whether the money is better spent elsewhere — or if you are already using it and need tools that cover the gaps — this guide breaks down nine alternatives by what they actually do.

## Why teams look for Metaview alternatives

Three patterns come up repeatedly:

1. **The real problem is screening, not note-taking.** Interview notes help after you have decided who to interview. If you are still buried in a 200-person applicant pool, notes on the eight you eventually talk to are a downstream optimisation.

2. **Pricing does not fit small teams.** Metaview charges per seat, and the value scales with interview volume. A two-person hiring team running three roles does not generate enough interviews to justify the cost.

3. **Teams want one fewer tool, not one more.** Adding a standalone note-taker means another login, another integration, another thing to maintain. Many teams would rather their ATS or screening tool handled it natively.

#### 2. BrightHire

BrightHire records interviews, generates transcripts, and creates structured highlights tied to your scorecard. It is closer to Metaview than any other tool on this list — same category, different execution.

**What it adds over Metaview:** Tighter ATS integration (especially with Greenhouse and Lever), interviewer coaching features, and compliance-focused recording consent flows.

**Best for:** Mid-size teams with a formal interview process who want structured evidence alongside their scorecards.

#### 3. Pillar

Pillar combines interview recording with an interview intelligence layer that scores interviewer consistency and flags potential bias in question patterns. It appeals to teams building equitable hiring processes at scale.

**Best for:** Teams with 10+ interviewers who want to standardise and measure interview quality.

### Interview intelligence and meeting tools

#### 4. Fireflies.ai

Fireflies is a general-purpose meeting assistant that records, transcribes, and summarises any meeting — not just interviews. It integrates with Zoom, Google Meet, and Teams, and offers a searchable transcript library.

**What it replaces:** Metaview's core recording and note-taking, but for every meeting type. The tradeoff is that it is not built specifically for hiring, so you lose recruiting-specific features like scorecard alignment.

**Best for:** Teams that want one tool for all meetings, not just interviews.

#### 5. Zoom AI Companion

If your interviews happen on Zoom, the built-in AI Companion generates summaries and action items without an additional tool. It is free on paid Zoom plans.

**What it replaces:** The basic note-taking function of Metaview — no third-party integration required.

**Best for:** Teams already paying for Zoom Business or Enterprise who want zero-setup meeting summaries.

#### 6. Otter.ai

Otter provides real-time transcription and searchable meeting archives. It sits between Fireflies and Zoom AI Companion in terms of specialisation — more powerful than built-in tools, less recruiting-specific than BrightHire.

**Best for:** Budget-conscious teams who need accurate transcription across all meetings.

### Async video screening

#### 7. Hireflix

Hireflix replaces live first-round interviews with one-way video questions. Candidates record answers on their own time, and you review them asynchronously. This eliminates the scheduling problem entirely for early-stage screening.

**What it replaces:** The interviews Metaview would have recorded. If the conversation does not need to be live, it does not need real-time note-taking either.

**Best for:** High-volume roles where a 15-minute live screen for every candidate is not feasible.

### ATS-native options

#### 8. Ashby

Ashby is a modern ATS with built-in analytics, scheduling, and structured interviewing. Its native interview scorecards and pipeline analytics reduce the need for bolt-on tools like Metaview.

**Best for:** Teams choosing a new ATS who want interview management built in rather than bolted on.

#### 9. Greenhouse

Greenhouse supports structured hiring out of the box — interview kits, scorecards, and an integration marketplace with 500+ tools. Many teams find that Greenhouse's native features plus one or two integrations cover what Metaview does.

**Best for:** Scaling teams that want a mature, extensible ATS with strong interviewing support.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Metaview worth it for a small team?

It depends on where your bottleneck is. If you are conducting 20+ interviews a month and losing time to note-taking, Metaview solves that. If your bottleneck is earlier — reading applications, shortlisting, or managing the pipeline — a tool like Fluxberry covers more of the workflow at a lower price.

### Can I use Metaview with any ATS?

Metaview integrates with most major ATS platforms including Greenhouse, Lever, and Ashby. However, the depth of integration varies. Check whether the features you need (scorecard sync, candidate linking) are supported for your specific ATS.

### What is the best free Metaview alternative?

Zoom AI Companion is free on paid Zoom plans and covers basic meeting summaries. For a free-tier screening and pipeline tool, [Fluxberry offers a free workspace](/sign-up) that includes AI scoring, pipeline management, and a careers page.

### Do I need interview recording if I have AI screening?

Not necessarily. AI screening (like Fluxberry's applicant scoring) reduces the number of interviews you conduct by surfacing the best candidates earlier. Fewer interviews means less note-taking to manage. Many small teams find that better screening eliminates the need for a recording tool entirely.
