## What makes a good ATS for a small team

We judged each applicant tracking system on six things that matter most when you don't have a recruiting team:

- **Time to first hire.** Can you post a job and collect applications the same day, or is there an implementation project first?
- **Screening help.** Does it help you read applications, and can you see why it recommends someone?
- **Price at your size.** What you pay at 10 and at 50 employees, and what's locked behind higher plans.
- **Everyone can join.** Whether hiring managers and interviewers cost extra.
- **The basics done well:** a careers page, job-board posting, a clear pipeline, interview scheduling and candidate email.
- **Getting out.** Whether you can export your candidates if you move on.

Prices and features come from each vendor's own pages, checked in September 2026. Several tools are demo-only, and we haven't used those ourselves; where that matters, we say so.

## What is an applicant tracking system?

An applicant tracking system (ATS) is software that manages hiring from job post to offer. It publishes your jobs, collects every application in one place, moves candidates through stages you define, schedules interviews, stores the team's feedback and keeps a record of every decision. Modern ATSs also use AI to read and prioritise applications.

## ATS features by hiring stage

![Six steps of an applicant tracking system: post, collect applications, parse and score, review the shortlist, interview, hire](/blog/best-applicant-tracking-systems/how-an-applicant-tracking-system-works.webp "Every candidate moves through the same six steps in one shared system.")

| Stage | What a good ATS does | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Attract | Careers page, posting to job boards, application forms | Can you brand the careers page? Which boards are included? |
| Screen | Parses resumes, scores or checks them against the job, flags duplicates and fraud | Can you see why a candidate is recommended? |
| Interview | Scheduling from your calendar, interview kits, scorecards, shared notes | Do interviewers need a paid seat? |
| Decide and hire | Offers, approvals, e-signature, hand-off to HR | How does a hire reach your HR or payroll system? |
| Improve | Reports on sources, time in stage and drop-off | Can you export the data? |

## How AI screening works, and what to ask vendors

"AI screening" now describes three quite different things:

- **Scoring and ranking.** The tool gives each applicant a score against the job and sorts the list. Fluxberry, Lever, Workable, Pinpoint, Breezy HR, Manatal and Gem all do this, and the better ones show the reasoning behind every score.
- **Criteria checks.** The tool marks each of your criteria as met or not met, without an overall score. Ashby and Recruitee work this way on purpose, to keep the ranking decision with a person.
- **Matching.** The tool prioritises applicants against weighted criteria or suggests candidates from your database, as Greenhouse's Talent Matching and Zoho's Zia Matches do.

None of these should make the hiring decision, and several vendors, including Greenhouse, Gem and Pinpoint, say theirs never advances or rejects anyone on its own. Before you switch one on, ask the vendor:

1. **What exactly does the score or match mean,** and can a recruiter see the reasoning for each candidate?
2. **What's removed before the AI sees a resume?** Several vendors redact personal details first.
3. **Has it been independently audited for bias,** and can you see a summary? Ashby, Greenhouse and Gem all cite third-party bias audits.
4. **Is our data used to train models?** Ashby and Pinpoint, for example, say they don't.
5. **How do we tell candidates?** Some laws require it.

Two laws matter most today. In New York City, [Local Law 144](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dca/about/automated-employment-decision-tools.page) requires a bias audit within a year before an automated tool is used to screen candidates, a published summary of that audit, and notice to candidates at least ten business days before use; fines run from $500 to $1,500 per violation. In the EU, the AI Act treats AI used to filter applications or evaluate candidates as [high-risk](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/annex-3), with duties for employers such as human oversight, monitoring and informing candidates; after a 2026 amendment those rules [apply from 2 December 2027](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/timeline/timeline-implementation-eu-ai-act). This isn't legal advice, so check your own obligations with a lawyer.

## How to choose an ATS

| Factor | What to consider |
|---|---|
| Hiring volume | A few hires a year needs a light tool; dozens of roles need automation and reporting |
| Who reviews applications | If it's a founder or manager with no time, AI screening with visible reasoning matters most |
| Interview process | If many interviewers run structured loops, prioritise interview kits and scorecards |
| Team access | Check whether hiring managers and interviewers cost extra |
| Price model | Work out the cost at today's size and at twice your headcount |
| Integrations | List your HRIS, calendar, background checks and assessments, and check each one |
| Try before you buy | Prefer tools with a trial, or ask for a sandbox on a real role |
| Data export | Confirm you can export candidates and resumes if you leave |

## How to set up an ATS in your first week

1. **Day 1:** Create your workspace, add your logo and publish your careers page.
2. **Day 1:** Set up your pipeline stages. Five is usually enough: applied, screened, interview, offer, hired.
3. **Day 2:** Recreate your open roles, with clear must-haves the screening can use.
4. **Day 2:** Connect your calendar and invite hiring managers.
5. **Day 3:** Write email templates for application received, interview invite and rejection.
6. **Days 4–5:** Move active candidates across, starting with anyone mid-interview.
7. **End of week:** Check where applications arrive from and what's still sitting unreviewed.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best ATS for a small business?

It depends on how you hire. For a small team that wants applications scored and explained at a flat price, Fluxberry. For structured interviews, Greenhouse. For analytics, Ashby. For job-board reach with a trial, Workable. For the lowest flat price among established tools, Breezy HR.

### How much does an applicant tracking system cost?

For a small business, published prices run from about $40 to $400 a month at 10 employees. Flat-priced tools like Fluxberry start at {{price:pro:monthly}}/month; Breezy HR from $157/month and Workable from $299/month billed annually; Ashby from $300/month; Greenhouse and Lever are quote-only.

### Do small companies need an ATS?

Once you have more than one or two open roles, or more applicants than you can reply to within a few days, yes. An ATS stops applications getting lost, gives every candidate a reply and lets the whole team see where each hire stands.

### What is the difference between an ATS and an HRIS?

An ATS manages candidates before they're hired: jobs, applications, interviews and offers. An HRIS (such as BambooHR) manages employees after they're hired: records, time off, payroll and benefits. Some HR suites include a basic ATS, and most ATSs hand new hires to an HRIS.

### Is AI resume screening fair?

It can be, if it's used as decision support. Look for tools that show the reasoning behind every score, strip personal details before scoring, have been independently audited for bias, and leave every decision to a person. Some places, such as New York City, require bias audits and candidate notice by law.

### What is the easiest ATS to set up?

Tools with self-serve sign-up and published pricing are quickest: Fluxberry, Breezy HR, Workable, Zoho Recruit and Manatal can all be running the same day. Demo-led products such as Greenhouse, Lever and Pinpoint typically need an implementation period.
