Most hiring tools are filing cabinets. They hold applications, they show you a list, and the
actual work — reading 200 résumés to find the eight worth a call — still lands on whoever has the
least protected calendar that week.

That work does not scale, and it does not stay fair. By résumé 150 you are skimming for reasons to
say no, and the last person in the pile gets a different process than the first.

## What we changed

Fluxberry scores every application the moment it arrives, against the role you actually wrote.
By the time you open the pipeline, each candidate carries a score, a short summary, and the
specific signals behind it.

The point is not the score. The point is that the reading has already happened, consistently, for
everyone — so the first thing you do is compare candidates instead of processing them.

### What a score is, and what it isn't

A score is a **suggestion attached to a candidate**, not a decision. Nothing advances a candidate
on its own. Stage changes are made by a person on your team, and the reasoning stays visible:

- the requirements the application matched
- the ones it missed
- a summary you can disagree with in one click

> The goal was never to decide who gets hired. It was to make sure nobody gets skipped because
> they applied on a Friday afternoon.

## Where the time actually goes

We measured a typical week for a two-person hiring team before and after, on the same role:

| Task | Before | With Fluxberry |
|---|---|---|
| Reading applications | ~6 hours | Review a ranked shortlist |
| Chasing assessment results | Manual follow-up | Auto-graded on submit |
| Booking interviews | Email tennis | Candidate picks a slot |
| Writing up feedback | Scattered notes | One shared scorecard |

The hours do not disappear — they move. Teams spend them on the four conversations that decide the
hire instead of the ninety that do not.

## How it fits together

1. A candidate applies through your careers page or a referral link.
2. Screening runs immediately and attaches a score and summary.
3. A workflow you configured once sends the assessment, or moves them to `Interview`.
4. Interview slots sync to Google Calendar; a scorecard opens when the call ends.
5. Analytics show where candidates drop out, by source and by stage.

Every step is something you can turn off. Teams that want to read every application still can —
they just start from a pile that has already been organised.
